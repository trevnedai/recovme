Your chances: good if a backup on your computer has them

Apple doesn’t let any app read an iPhone or iPad directly, so no app can scan its memory for deleted photos. What RecovMe can do is read the backups that Finder, iTunes or the Apple Devices app made on your computer. A backup from before the photos were deleted still has them, including photos that were in Recently Deleted.

Before you do anything

  • Don’t back up the iPhone again yet. A new backup replaces the old one, and the old one may be the only copy of your photos.
  • Archive the old backup first if you need to make a new one: in Finder (or iTunes, or Apple Devices), choose Manage Backups, right-click the backup and choose Archive.
  • Don’t restore the iPhone from an old backup to get photos back. Restoring replaces everything that’s on it now.

Check these places first

  • Recently Deleted on the iPhone. In Photos, open Albums › Recently Deleted. Photos stay there for about 30 days.
  • iCloud Photos. If iCloud Photos was on, sign in at icloud.com and look in Photos and in its Recently Deleted album.

What RecovMe brings back from a backup

  • The Camera Roll with original names. Photos that were in Recently Deleted and in the Hidden album each get their own folder.
  • Previews of photos kept only in iCloud, when the backup has one.
  • Messages that were in Messages › Recently Deleted (iOS 16 and later), as a text file, with their photos.
  • Messages and WhatsApp attachments, voice memos with their titles, Notes attachments and app documents.

Encrypted backups work too. RecovMe asks for the backup password and never saves it.

Recover from a backup in 5 steps

RecovMe only reads the backup; it doesn’t change it. It’s free to the end: no trial, no limit, nothing to pay before you can save.

  1. Open RecovMe on the computer with the backup

    Download it free for Mac or Windows. Backups made by Finder, iTunes or Apple Devices on that computer appear by themselves under Phones & Tablets in the list on the left. A backup on another drive can be opened with Open a Disk Image or Backup….

  2. Pick the backup

    Choose the one from before the photos were deleted. RecovMe shows what it can and can’t bring back before the first scan.

  3. Enter the password if the backup is encrypted

    Under Backup Password, type the password you chose when you turned on “Encrypt local backup”. It’s not the iPhone’s passcode. It is only used on this computer and never saved.

  4. Choose what to find and where to save

    Under Look For, keep Photos and Videos on and add what else you need. Under Save Recovered Files To, pick a folder with enough free space.

  5. Start and open your files

    Press Start Scan, then Open Recovered Files when it’s done.

Questions

Can any app scan an iPhone for deleted photos without a backup?

No. iOS doesn’t let any computer app read the iPhone’s storage directly, and its storage is encrypted. Apps that claim to do it read backups or iCloud too. Without a backup made before the deletion, Recently Deleted and iCloud are the only places to look.

Where are iPhone backups stored?

On a Mac, in your user Library under Application Support › MobileSync › Backup. On Windows, under your user folder in Apple › MobileSync › Backup (or AppData › Roaming › Apple Computer › MobileSync › Backup with the older iTunes). RecovMe finds them by itself.

Can it read iCloud backups?

No. RecovMe reads backups stored on your computer. iCloud backups can only be used by restoring an iPhone, which replaces what’s on it.

Does it change my backup?

No. It only reads the backup and copies files out of it.

Is RecovMe really free?

Yes, 100%. There’s no trial, no size limit and nothing to unlock after the scan. It’s open source, and donations are optional.
