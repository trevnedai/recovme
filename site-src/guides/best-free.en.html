Short answer

Most “free” data recovery apps let you scan for free, then cap what you can save at 100 MB to 2 GB. Only a few have no limit: PhotoRec, Microsoft’s Windows File Recovery and RecovMe, plus some free tools limited to certain files or drives. The full table, checked on each vendor’s website, is below.

Written by Dhan Denvert Asuncion, who makes RecovMe, so read it with that in mind. Every limit links to the vendor’s own page, checked on 30 October 2026. Free limits change often; Disk Drill’s has changed several times.

Which one should you use?

  • Mac with Apple silicon, and you want a normal app: RecovMe. On a Mac, it’s the only one here that recovers without a limit and without Terminal.
  • Linux or an Intel Mac: PhotoRec.
  • Windows, and you’re comfortable with the command line: Windows File Recovery, from Microsoft.
  • Windows, files deleted minutes ago from a hard drive: Recuva or RecovMe’s Quick Scan, both of which keep the original names.
  • A drive that clicks, isn’t detected or keeps disconnecting: none of them. Unplug it and use a data recovery lab if the files matter.

What no free or paid app can do

No app can bring back files from the internal SSD of a modern laptop or an Apple silicon Mac once they’re deleted: the drive wipes them by itself, and Macs encrypt it. Phones are similar: only copies that still exist, in a trash folder, a chat app or a backup, can come back. Be wary of any app that promises otherwise. For those devices, check the Trash, Time Machine, File History, OneDrive, iCloud or Google Photos first.

Questions

Why do so many “free” recovery apps have a limit?

The free version is a trial: it shows you what it found so you’ll buy the paid version to save more. RecovMe, PhotoRec and Windows File Recovery are free without that catch.

Is free recovery software safe?

Download it only from the maker’s own website. A good recovery app only reads the drive it scans and saves to a different drive, so it can’t overwrite what you want back.

Are open-source recovery tools as good as paid ones?

For deleted and formatted files on cards and drives, PhotoRec is widely used by professionals, and The Sleuth Kit is a standard forensics tool. RecovMe runs both. Paid apps add extras such as drive backups or support, not a different kind of magic.
